Sequence vs. Bellman

Computational Macro · Session 1

The same growth model, log utility with production kα and full depreciation, solved twice. Route one hunts for the single saving rate that survives transversality. Route two iterates an operator until it stops moving. Both land on k′ = αβ kα. Nothing here needs installing. Move a slider and the charts, the numbers and the verdict line all update as you go.

The Euler equation collapses to a first‑order difference equation in the saving rate st = kt+1 / ktα. Capital drops out, and with it the initial condition. Every starting value s0 ∈ (0,1) satisfies the Euler equation. Only one of them is optimal.

s t+1 = φ(st) = 1 − αβ · (1 − st) / st Fixed points where φ(s) = s: exactly s = αβ and s = 1. Slope φ′(s) = αβ/s², so φ′(αβ) = 1/αβ > 1 (repelling) and φ′(1) = αβ < 1 (attracting).

Snap s₀ onto the knife edge, then nudge it by one tick. The Euler equation cannot tell the two apart, transversality can.

Now drag s₀ from one side of αβ to the other and read the verdict line. Two different things go wrong, and only one of them is about transversality.

Numerics —

Same model, unknowns are now two functions of the state rather than an infinite sequence. Guess v(k) = E + F ln k and the whole problem reduces to one number, F. Iterating the Bellman operator becomes iterating a scalar, which is small enough to watch converge.

B1Iterate the coefficient, from any starting guess

With vn(k) = Fn ln k + Dn, one Bellman update gives k′ = βFn/(1+βFn) · kα and Fn+1 = α + αβ Fn. The contraction theorem says the starting guess cannot matter. Test it.

Pick any starting value, the more absurd the better. Pin it, then pin another. Read off Fn after 25 iterations each time and compare the digits.

Worth stopping on

The contraction theorem promises ‖vn − v*‖ ≤ βn‖v0 − v*‖, a rate of β = 0.96. The chart shows the error falling by a factor of 0.288 = αβ each step, far faster. Is the theorem wrong?

(No. β is a worst case over the whole function space, and this family of log functions is not the worst case. It also is not in the bounded function space the theorem is stated on. Both threads lead into the week‑3 trivia topic on why the Bellman operator is a contraction.)

B2The policy function, iteration by iteration

Each Fn implies a saving rate σn = βFn/(1+βFn), and the policy k′ = σn kα climbs onto αβ kα. Note where these numbers land. σ∞ = αβ, exactly the knife-edge saving rate the other route had to argue for.

The question to leave with

Push the tolerance as low as you like. The iteration error falls to 10⁻⁹ and the red line still flattens out well above zero. Where does the error that remains come from, and would running more iterations ever remove it?

Now move the grid slider and watch that floor move instead. This is the reason every method in this course is first run on a model we can solve by hand.

Parameters default to the deck's α = 0.30, β = 0.96, so αβ = 0.288. Everything is computed in the page. The grid solver is brute-force value function iteration over N×N capital choices, stopping at ‖v_n − v_{n−1}‖ < 1e−9. The analytical benchmark is v(k) = E + α/(1−αβ)·ln k with E = [ln(1−αβ) + βα/(1−αβ)·ln(αβ)]/(1−β).
